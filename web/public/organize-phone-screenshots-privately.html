How to organize phone screenshots without uploading private data

Open the Screenshots album on almost any phone and you will find a mix of recipes, flight confirmations, clinic receipts, bank transfer confirmations, and half-remembered memes. Taking a screenshot takes half a second, so your brain treats it as a quick way to remember something.

Why cloud screenshot organizers feel uncomfortable

Many AI note apps ask you to upload every image to their remote servers so they can run OCR and tagging. That is fine for a pasta recipe, but most people do not want dentist bills, prescriptions, or private messages stored on a third-party server just to remember a reimbursement deadline.

Process private screenshots on the device

Modern phones and desktop Chrome now support on-device models like Gemini Nano and Apple Foundation Models. Tuck runs summarization and tagging locally first, and enforces a database rule that blocks private screenshot image data from ever being written to cloud Firestore.

Keep your camera roll clean

Instead of letting 2,000 screenshots sit untouched, share the useful ones to Tuck from your Android or iOS Share Sheet. Each morning, review three items, finish the ones with real deadlines, and delete the rest.

Triage screenshots privately with Tuck

Mark any receipt or personal note as private to keep its image strictly local while still getting a clear summary and next step.
