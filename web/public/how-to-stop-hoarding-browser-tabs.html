How to stop hoarding browser tabs without losing useful links

Most people keep 40 or 80 Chrome tabs open for the same reason: closing a tab feels like forgetting it forever. Bookmarks rarely help because once a page lands in a folder called "Read Later," you almost never open that folder again.

Why bookmark folders turn into black holes

Bookmarking creates two kinds of friction. First, you have to pick a folder or invent a tag while you are in the middle of something else. Second, nothing reminds you to look back at what you saved. After a few weeks, the folder holds 200 vague page titles and opening it feels like homework.

Save the reason, then close the tab

A raw URL rarely tells you why you cared about a page nine days later. If you save a Kyoto ramen thread from Reddit, what you actually need is one line: "Menya Inoichi Hanare opens at 11am, get a ticket at 10:15am, cash only." Once that summary is captured, the open tab has done its job and you can close it right away.

Review three forgotten saves a day

Trying to clean 100 tabs on a Sunday afternoon almost always fails. Reviewing just three older items a day takes under two minutes. For each one, make a quick call: act on it now, keep it on your shelf, or let it go.
