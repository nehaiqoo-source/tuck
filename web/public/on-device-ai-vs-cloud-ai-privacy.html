On-device AI vs cloud AI: what belongs locally and what needs the cloud

Sending every tiny prompt to a cloud API is slow, costs money on every click, and forces users to ship personal text over the network. Running everything locally has its own limit: small on-device models cannot easily combine ten separate documents into a detailed plan.

What runs on-device in Tuck

Every time you tuck a tab, note, or screenshot, Tuck calls the Firebase AI Logic SDK in PREFER_ON_DEVICE mode. On supported Chrome and Android devices, Gemini Nano generates a short title, summary, and next step right on the hardware, even with Wi-Fi turned off. On iOS, it targets Apple Foundation Models.

When Cloud Gemini steps in

Cloud fallback happens in two places. First, if a user opens Tuck on a browser or phone without a local model, Firebase AI Logic automatically routes the request to gemini-3.5-flash-lite so the app still works with zero setup. Second, when you select several saved items on your shelf and click "Connect dots in Cloud," Tuck explicitly uses ONLY_IN_CLOUD mode to merge those saves into one ordered plan.
